---

### Value Prop 1 — *Replaces "Autonomous Delivery"*

**Works While You Work**

You shouldn't have to monitor markets, track competitors, or comb through sources. Your harness does it — continuously, in the background, calibrated to your role. By the time you need the intelligence, it's already waiting.

### Value Prop 2 — *Replaces "On-Demand Depth"*

**Knows When You Need to Go Deep**

Surface-level summaries aren't enough for the decisions you make. Ask Frontier anything and it researches — not retrieves. Cross-source analysis, verified claims, connected context across domains. The depth a senior analyst would give you, delivered in your workflow.

The longer you work with Frontier, the sharper it gets. It learns your frameworks, your decision patterns, the signals you consistently act on and the ones you ignore. Over time, it stops behaving like a tool and starts operating like a colleague who's been in every room with you.

## HOW IT WORKS — Activation Journey

*Frame: not "how to set up the product" but "how the harness comes to life"*

---

### Step 1 — Calibrate Your Role

**Tell it who you are and how you think.**

Set your role, your focus areas, and the questions that keep you up at night. This isn't onboarding — it's calibration. Your harness needs to understand your work before it can amplify it. The more honest you are here, the sharper it gets from day one.

---

### Step 2 — Teach It Your Signal

**Shape it as it learns.**

As Frontier surfaces intelligence, you direct it — react, redirect, push deeper. Every signal you engage with teaches your harness more about what matters to someone in your exact position. It gets sharper every week. Most users feel a meaningful shift within the first month.

---

### Step 3 — Work With an AI That Knows You

**From here, it works alongside you.**

Your harness delivers briefings before you have to ask, goes deep when the decision demands it, and connects signals across domains you'd never have time to monitor yourself. This isn't a tool you open. It's a colleague that's already been at it.

## COPY NOTES & RATIONALE

**Voice shift:** The current copy is feature-narration ("Frontier delivers autonomous intelligence briefings"). The new copy is role-narration — Frontier is always described in terms of what it does for *your work*, not what it does as a product.

**"Harness" framing:** Used sparingly but consistently — in the headline, CTA, and value prop intros. The word carries the right semantic load (fitted, amplifying, persistent) without needing explanation. Avoid overusing it in body copy.

**Role-switcher micro-copy:** Each one ends with a role-specific outcome, not a product feature. Design Lead → room credibility. Strategy → agenda ownership. Investor → pre-consensus edge. These are the real purchase motivators for each segment.

**CTA: "Activate Your Harness"** — signals relationship, not subscription. Implies the harness is waiting to be tuned to you, not that you're signing up for access to a database. Consider A/B testing against "Build Your Intelligence Layer" for a more tangible alternative.

**How It Works:** Each step is written from the user's emotional state, not the product's technical state. Step 1 is about self-knowledge, Step 2 is about collaboration, Step 3 is about trust. This mirrors how any real colleague relationship develops.

**What's not said:** Nowhere does the copy mention "briefings delivered to your inbox" or "sources indexed" or "AI-powered summaries." These are table stakes and they position the product as a content service. The harness concept demands that we describe *what kind of professional you become* when you use it, not what the product technically does.
